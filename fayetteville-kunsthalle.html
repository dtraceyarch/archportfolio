Fayetteville Kunsthalle

Fall 2023 · Public Works · Fayetteville, Arkansas

The Fayetteville Kunsthalle project unfolds in three stages, beginning with the study of a common roof form and ending in a full architectural program positioned on a steep hill near downtown Fayetteville — part of the city's expanding Cultural Arts Corridor.

Process
Stage 1

Roof Form Study

Assigned the barn roof type, the team analyzes structure, spatial organization, and the qualities of natural light, using the LSU Ag. Barn as a reference for scale and proportion.

Stage 2

Roof Transformation & Refinement

The barn roof is transformed based on a verb list, with attention to how light interacts with the new form. Concepts are refined through paper models tested under various daylight conditions, and structural strategies explore how the form meets the ground.

Stage 3

Full Program & Site Integration

The transformed roof is applied to a full program — gallery spaces, a café, employee areas, and a double-height exhibition hall. A double-shell system modulates light: an inner PTFE fabric shell emits a soft glow, while a dark stained wood outer shell filters light through narrow gaps, with a custom truss system between the shells for structure and maintenance access. The larger form holds the main gallery and sculptural niches; the smaller form holds a café above and offices below. A linear plaza between the volumes supports circulation and community use, and green terraces step down the slope to meet South Street. A future park on the west side connects the Kunsthalle to the Ramble, reinforcing its role as a public anchor in the corridor.

Gallery

Overview. The Kunsthalle treats the barn roof less as a historical reference and more as a light-modulating instrument — the double-shell system, PTFE inner layer, and slatted outer wood shell were all developed through iterative daylight studies and physical models before being applied to the final gallery, café, and exhibition program.
