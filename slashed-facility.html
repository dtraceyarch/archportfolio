Slashed Facility

Spring 2025 · Public Works & Institutional · New York City, NY

SLASHED is a regenerative academic and community facility designed for the Manhattanville neighborhood of Harlem, New York City. Submitted to the AIA COTE Top Ten for Students competition, the project challenges conventional institutional architecture by integrating ecological systems, biophilic strategies, and urban connectivity into a unified design response. The concept is organized around a "slashed" vertical atrium that draws light, air, and movement through the building, connecting design studios, labs, exhibition space, an auditorium, and community and local-craft program.

The structure uses mass timber to lower embodied carbon and support occupant well-being through exposed natural materials, paired with biophilic strategies — daylight access, indoor planting, and visual connections to green space. Flexibility is built into the framework, with the majority of the building's components designed for reuse or disassembly. Green roofs, bioswales, operable shading, and passive ventilation cut annual energy use and capture significant rainwater on site, while landscape interventions restore habitat and raise site biodiversity.

Overview. SLASHED proposes a future-forward model for academic architecture that emphasizes environmental performance, social inclusion, and long-term adaptability within the context of dense urban development. Private study spaces, labs, and exhibition areas are arranged around the central atrium so daylight and circulation reach deep into the plan, while ground-floor community and craft spaces extend engagement out toward the street and plaza.
