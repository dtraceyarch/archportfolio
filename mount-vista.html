Mount Vista

Fall 2024 · Residential · Los Angeles, California · Professor Pierce McVey

Mount Vista is a three-stage study of a single lot in the Mar Vista neighborhood of Los Angeles, originally designed by Gregory Ain to accommodate the everyday postwar family through a set of repeating, efficient floorplans. The first stage documented the existing lot and its neighborhood logic; the second reimagined it as a family compound; the third — Stepping Stones — pushed the same site toward the density a growing city now asks of it.

Stage two introduced two accessory dwelling units into the backyard of the existing Ain house, built for the owner's parents and cousins to move onto the property. Each unit had to work within Los Angeles's ADU zoning code, including maximum buildable area, while still reading as part of a coherent family compound rather than two unrelated add-ons.

Stage three, Stepping Stones, replaced part of the surrounding Mar Vista fabric with a multifamily complex designed with a project partner, responding to a new streetcar line along the main road and the area's growing appetite for infill density. Unlike the single-client scenario of stage two, Stepping Stones had to serve two distinct populations at once — multigenerational households and supportive housing residents — while preserving or re-routing existing throughfares and holding to zoning limits including FAR. Additional commercial space and public amenity were left open to the design.

Overview. Mount Vista reads a single Gregory Ain lot at three different scales of intervention — an ADU addition sized to a family's real needs, then a multifamily complex sized to a neighborhood's future — testing how the same postwar logic of repeatable, efficient plans can absorb new density without losing what made the original block work.
