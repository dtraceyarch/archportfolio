Momentum STL

Spring 2024 · Public Works · St. Louis, Missouri

Momentum is a performing arts center focused on rebuilding communal bonds in the St. Louis metro area through redevelopment. A black-box and outdoor theater form the "heart" of the building around which users circulate, with a seating area adjacent to every rehearsal space for interaction beyond the performers themselves.

Vertical circulation runs primarily along a "snake" that extrudes out from the primary form along both 22nd and Olive Streets and is visible from the street — a direct reference to the Delmar Divide, and to the idea that residents to the north still have a path to upward mobility through new communal projects that reconnect the city's split urban fabric. A secondary exterior patio rests above the snake as a spillout space that engages passersby, while a skylight over the lobby lights the entry to the black-box theater, the building's centerpiece. The lobby itself opens upward, giving the building a tall vertical spillout that spans the full height of the structure.

Overview. Momentum reads the Delmar Divide as a design problem as much as a social one — the "snake" circulation spine is the building's most legible move, pulling movement out to the street on both 22nd and Olive so the building's activity is visible and porous rather than closed off. The black-box theater, physical models, and site renders all study how a single civic anchor can knit a fractured block back together.
